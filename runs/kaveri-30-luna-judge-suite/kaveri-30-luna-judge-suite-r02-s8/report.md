# House On Kaveri Lane - Audience Simulation Report

## 1. Verdict Card

- **Recommendation:** Major rewrite; confidence medium-low.
- **Retention:** The series falls from a retained index of **100.0** after Episode 1 to **0.0** after Episode 8.
- **Primary break:** Episode 4 reaches a retained index of **53.3** after the largest mid-season loss; Episode 8 then loses all remaining listeners.
- **Core diagnosis:** Specific threat hooks retain listeners briefly, but repeated payoff delay lowers trust. The strongest emotional reversals—Episode 5’s body reveal and knife threat—restore craving temporarily; Episode 8’s successful cleanup and routine ending convert tension into closure.
- **Paywall read:** No ending creates strong paid pressure. Episode 1 is the best candidate, but its simulated pay rate is only **13.3%**.

- **Justice-payoff binger:** Drops to **16.7** after Episode 4 and **0.0** after Episode 7. Agents repeatedly described delayed justice, proof, or accountability as outweighing the cliffhangers.

## 4. Drop-Beat Inspector

- **Episode 4 — `s004_b06`, “The Backyard Search Threat”:** 9 listeners dropped; the largest loss included 3 high-churn thrill chasers and 3 justice-payoff bingers. Felt emotions were “frustrated curiosity” and “payoff fatigue.” The judgement bridge: a future warrant search could expose evidence, but after three unresolved episodes it was perceived as another postponement rather than proof or justice.
- **Episode 6 — `s006_b05`, “The call becomes violence”:** 6 listeners dropped. The assault briefly raised tension, then emotion shifted to shock, discomfort, and reduced sympathy. Agents said Tara’s witness status was a real hook, but the tonal break, weak payoff trust, and resolved-feeling ending outweighed it for several regions.
- **Episode 8 — `s008_b06`, “The River Takes the Evidence”:** 3 listeners dropped at the beat; all 8 active listeners ultimately left the episode. Craving fell sharply when the evidence vanished without interruption and Tara said, “It’s over.” The judgement bridge identifies completed cleanup as the decisive counterweight to speculative future exposure.
- **Episode 8 — `s008_b08`, “Grading After the Cover-Up”:** 3 Slow-burn comfort seekers dropped. Relief and family reassurance were followed by flatness and tonal dissonance when routine grading replaced suspense; the emotional payoff was self-contained and did not open a next-play need.
- **Episode 3 — `s003_b06`:** 4 listeners dropped across four regions. The accusation raised suspicion, but emotion shifted from tension to underwhelm because the story had delivered three escalating threats without justice, catharsis, or a meaningful reveal.

## 5. Paywall Map

- **Do not gate Episode 2.** The police confrontation releases without arrest or confession; the phone reveal earns free continuation but not payment.
- **Episodes 3–4:** **0.0%** paid. The emotional/conflict gates are undermined by unresolved clues and delayed proof.
- **Episode 5:** **12.5%** paid, but only after the body mystery pays off and the knife threat creates immediate danger. This is still weak pressure, not a reliable gate.
- **Episode 6:** No clean gate; the ending is “Get another tarp,” with no simulated payment.

- **Episode 8:** No paid pressure. The resolved ending offers no reveal, public proof, irreversible consequence, or new threat.

## 6. Expectation Scorecard

- **Opening hook:** Fired. Episode 1 retained index is **100.0**; the aggressive arrival and missing phone create immediate curiosity.
- **Mid-season endurance:** Fired narrowly by the stated check, but structurally weak: Episode 4 is the main attrition point at retained index **53.3**.
- **Clean paid-gate shape:** Partial. Episode 1 is the best gate, but its **13.3%** pay rate remains weak.
- **Final retention threshold:** Missed. Final retained index is **0.0**.
- **Structural expectation failure:** Threats repeatedly raise craving, but delayed answers reduce payoff trust. Episode 5 is the exception because its body reveal supplies a concrete payoff before the next threat.

## 7. Panel Voice

- **What worked:** Agents consistently felt sharp suspense, dread, and urgent curiosity around named confrontations, physical evidence, the body reveal, and Meenakshi’s active defense of Tara.
- **What broke trust:** “Another postponement” recurs across Episodes 3, 4, and 7. The panel wanted justice, proof, accountability, emotional repair, or a meaningful power result—not only another investigation step.
- **Mixed reaction to violence:** Episode 6’s assault was “shocking rather than satisfying.” Tara’s witness role preserved curiosity for some listeners, but the tonal break damaged sympathy for others.
- **Ending problem:** Episode 8’s relief lowered rather than sustained craving. The bridge disposal and grading line made the story feel complete without a concrete Episode 9 gate.

## 8. Editorial Actions

- Rewrite **Episode 8 first**, beginning at `s008_b06`: do not let evidence removal resolve the active danger without interruption or consequence.
- Replace the routine grading close with an immediate, specific next-play pressure tied to the unresolved investigation or family consequence.
- Rebuild **Episode 4** so the warrant threat delivers proof, accountability, or an irreversible discovery rather than another delayed search.
- Reframe **Episode 6** to prepare the violence more clearly and preserve sympathy; retain Tara’s witness consequence as the forward engine.
- Use Episode 5 as the structural model: deliver a concrete carried mystery payoff, then launch a sharply defined threat.
- Hold the paywall until the episode promises a reveal, public proof, or irreversible consequence. Current simulated pressure is weak.
- Rerun an LLM pass on a smaller panel for qualitative comparison; this output is uncalibrated and directional.

- Cohort: India/English Pocket-FM Listener Panel; population 30; 8 episodes.

- The structural prior and LLM judgement were broadly aligned, with strongest agreement on Episode 8’s complete drop.
- Absolute retention, payment, and craving values are not backtested against real listening, conversion, or completion logs.
