# House On Kaveri Lane - Audience Simulation Report

## 1. Verdict Card

- **Recommendation:** Major rewrite.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low; output is uncalibrated and directional.
- **Retention shape:** Strong opening, then cumulative attrition: retained index falls from **100.0** through Episodes 1–2 to **60.0** after Episode 4, **40.0** after Episode 6, **26.7** after Episode 7, and **0.0** after Episode 8.
- **Primary break:** Episode 8’s resolved ending converts tension into finality. All 8 remaining listeners dropped; end craving delta was **-4.625**.
- **Secondary breaks:** Episode 4’s indirect opening and Episode 6’s repeated concealment loop.
- **Core diagnosis:** Threat and cliffhangers generate curiosity, but repeated payoff delay lowers payoff trust. Agents repeatedly described **frustrated curiosity**, **impatience**, or **relief that felt like an ending**, not a reason to continue.

- **Slow-burn comfort seeker:** Falls to **40.0** after Episode 4 and **20.0** after Episode 6, then **0.0** after Episode 8. Emotional warmth or relief was repeatedly displaced by threat escalation; the closing reassurance felt complete rather than inviting.
- **Status-progression loyalist:** Falls to **60.0** after Episode 4, **40.0** after Episode 6, then **0.0** after Episode 8. Agents cited low visible progression and a weak next-play gate.

## 4. Drop-Beat Inspector

- **s008_b06 — “Vanished in the Current” (6 drops):** The disposal scene produces a brief thriller rush, then Tara says, “It’s over.” Emotion shifts from tension to relief and finality. Judgement bridges say the active danger is resolved without a concrete next-episode threat; the grading tag adds tonal abruptness rather than a usable hook.
- **s004_b01 — “The Bleach Question” (8 drops):** The episode opens with indirect questioning and institutional dismissal. Agents felt frustration because another investigation obstacle followed three episodes of unresolved danger. Craving later rose with the warrant, but the weak opening gate and low payoff trust won.
- **s006_b01 — “The Tarp Is Exposed” (4 drops; s006_b09 added 2):** The attack creates shock, then the tarp command extends the concealment loop. Agents recognized Tara’s witness dilemma as a specific hook, but several judged it weaker than the accumulated need for accountability, catharsis, or another clear payoff.
- **s007_b07 — “The Clean Backyard” (4 drops):** Meenakshi’s counterattack creates satisfaction and a revenge rush; the clean search result shifts that emotion into irritation and disappointment. The central disappearance remains unresolved, reinforcing payoff-delay fatigue.

## 5. Paywall Map

- **Best candidate:** Episode 5, “The Muddy Shoes,” at **11.1%** pay rate, with five close calls. It combines a concrete burial discovery with an immediate knife-driven confrontation.
- **Still weak:** The paywall verdict remains weak across Episodes 1–5 and 7. Threat gates earned free continuation but generally lacked delivered proof, justice, or a reliable payoff pattern.
- **Do not gate:** Episode 6 has no clean gate; Episode 8 is a resolved-no-gate comfort epilogue. Episode 8’s **0.0%** pay rate confirms that the ending did not create paid urgency.
- **Editorial implication:** Delay payment until an ending blocks a concrete reveal, public proof, irreversible consequence, or clearly promised confrontation—not merely another threat escalation.

## 7. Panel Voice

- **What worked:** Agents repeatedly praised immediate, legible danger: the aggressive knock, the phone under the sofa, the bleach accusation, the warrant, and the knife order. Felt emotions were typically **adrenaline**, **dread**, and **urgent curiosity**.
- **What broke trust:** “Another investigation obstacle,” “repeated unresolved mysteries,” and “another concealment task” were recurring judgement bridges. Mid-episode craving often rose, but end craving failed to convert when no proof, justice, accountability, or emotional repair followed.
- **Ending reaction:** “The story felt finished,” “cold and tonally abrupt,” “underwhelmed,” and “bas, story khatam” capture the Episode 8 response bucket.
- **Segment sensitivity:** Episode 8 lost **5/5 high-interruption** listeners, **4/4 social-short-clip** listeners, and **3/3 High-churn thrill chasers**. Episode 7 lost **3/3 Justice-payoff bingers**.

## 8. Editorial Actions

1. **Rewrite Episode 8 first.** Replace the resolved disposal/reassurance beat at s008_b06 with a concrete forward gate: immediate exposure, proof, justice movement, or a consequential Tara choice.
2. **Repair Episode 4’s opening.** Start with action or a delivered consequence before the indirect bleach exchange; the later warrant cannot reliably compensate for the weak gate.
3. **Reduce payoff delay.** Pay off at least one major investigation question before adding another accusation, procedural detail, or concealment task.
4. **Clarify the intended reward lane.** The mixed panel received danger but not a consistently legible blend of justice, catharsis, relationship movement, competence, or progression.
5. **Rework Episode 6’s ending.** Preserve the shock and Tara witness dilemma, but make the next consequence more immediate than “Get another tarp.”
6. **Do not use the current endings as paid gates.** Episode 5 is the only directional candidate, and even there the simulated conversion is limited.
7. **Rerun a smaller LLM panel after edits.** The structural prior and LLM judgement agreed strongly on Episode 8, but disagreed materially on Episodes 3–4; compare qualitative reasons rather than treating percentages as calibrated forecasts.
