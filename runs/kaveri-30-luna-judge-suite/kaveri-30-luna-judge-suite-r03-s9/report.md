## 4. Drop-Beat Inspector

- **Primary break: Episode 3, `s003_b10` — “The Bleach Question.”** It accounts for all 21 Episode 3 drops. The episode moves from confession and cover-up tension to another unanswered accusation. Agents repeatedly describe **frustrated curiosity** rather than satisfied excitement; judgement bridges cite payoff trust at **0.22**, low agency trust, and repeated delay.
- **Segment impact:** High-churn thrill chasers and justice-payoff bingers both reach a **0.0 retained index** after Episode 3. High-interruption listeners show a **76.9% drop rate** in the episode.
- **Secondary break: Episode 8, `s008_b01` — “The Silent Drive.”** The opening supplies only engine sound and silence. All 7 active listeners leave the episode; five drops are assigned to this beat.
- **Episode 8 finish: `s008_b07` — “Grading Before Monday.”** The burial and cleanup create catharsis, then the shift to ordinary grading produces **tonal whiplash, discomfort, and deflation**. Two Household catharsis devotees leave here.
- **Smaller warning:** Episode 5’s knife order (`s005_b07`) raises craving but is judged as another delayed consequence; one Slow-burn comfort seeker leaves.

## 5. Paywall Map

- **Do not gate Episodes 1–3 or 5:** pay pressure is weak, with pay rates of **3.3%, 6.9%, 3.4%, and 12.5%**. The agents consistently accept another free play but reject payment because danger is not yet a concrete paid promise.
- **Episode 4:** possible soft gate; **25.0% pay rate** and moderate pressure. Strengthen the threatened backyard search before charging.
- **Episode 7:** best candidate; **28.6% pay rate** and moderate pressure. The mother-versus-mother leverage reversal and empty-backyard question create the clearest paid curiosity, but the ending still delays the central answer.
- **Episode 6 and Episode 8:** do not gate. Both end with weak or unclear gates and no simulated payment.
- **Required gate shape:** block a concrete reveal, public proof, irreversible consequence, or meaningful relationship movement—not another general threat.

## 7. Panel Voice

- **Continuation voice:** “The aggressive knock,” “the hidden phone,” and “the backyard search” are repeatedly described as specific, easy-to-track next-play hooks. Felt emotions are **dread, anxious curiosity, and nervous suspense**.
- **Drop voice:** “Another accusation,” “another unanswered question,” and “another crisis escalation” recur across the Episode 3 opinions. Felt emotions shift to **frustrated curiosity** and frustration that an answer or consequence is postponed.
- **Survivor voice before Episode 8:** Meenakshi’s active protection of Tara and the leverage reversal generate satisfaction and agency interest. The clean backyard remains a usable question, but not a reliable paid promise.
- **Finale voice:** The burial reveal and cleanup are shocking and cathartic; the ending then produces **uneasy relief, tonal discomfort, anticlimax, and deflation** rather than a reason to resume.

## 8. Editorial Actions

1. **Rewrite Episode 3 first:** replace the unanswered bleach accusation with a delivered consequence, confrontation, discovery, or agency win. This is the largest retention break and removes 21 listeners.
2. **Rewrite Episode 8’s opening:** eliminate the silent-drive delay at `s008_b01`; begin with immediate verbal context or an active consequence.
3. **Rebuild Episode 8’s final movement:** preserve the cleanup/catharsis, but add a specific next-episode threat or unresolved emotional consequence. Rework the grading line so the tonal shift does not cancel the preceding payoff.
4. **Reduce serial threat repetition:** by Episode 3, agents report three escalation-heavy episodes without visible justice, exposure, or consequence. Resolve one investigation thread before adding another.
5. **Clarify Episode 4’s extra threads:** the money, blackmail, and exam-paper material raises confusion; prioritize the backyard search and family-protection conflict.
6. **Delay payment until the gate is concrete:** Episode 7 is the only viable candidate in this run, and it still needs a sharper reveal or irreversible consequence.
7. **Backtest the rewrite:** rerun a smaller LLM panel and compare qualitative drop reasons; the current run reports 30 LLM drops, 36 advisory flags, and only directional calibration.
